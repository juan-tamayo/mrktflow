# Module: fulfillment

Objective: ship physical goods and deliver purchased files with separate, accurate lifecycle controls. Depends on platform, identity, catalog and checkout-orders. Inherits [commerce rules](docs/commerce-rules.md), [data model](docs/data-model.md), and [commands](docs/coding-handoff.md).

## Requirements

- **FUL-01:** Staff selects paid unfulfilled physical quantities and records carrier/tracking/manual tracking URL. Validate URL scheme/domain policy and prevent overshipment; partial/multiple shipments supported.
- **FUL-02:** Shipment creation atomically consumes the correct stock allocation and emits notification intent. A retried request cannot decrement stock twice. Cancellation of a shipment record does not automatically restock goods already dispatched.
- **FUL-03:** Return records distinguish approval, receipt and restock from refunds. Explicit quantities and reasons; returned stock is added exactly once.
- **FUL-04:** Currently eligible paid digital lines issue unique version-pinned entitlements; stale paid-event replay after refund cannot grant access. Eligible lines show downloads in verified guest/customer order areas. Mixed orders unlock digital access independently of physical shipping.
- **FUL-05:** Private files require current entitlement authorization and bounded single-use download grants with a five-minute start window and separately authorized one-hour resumable transfer. Refund/dispute/expiry rules revoke or suspend future access; download counters are concurrency-safe.
- **FUL-06:** Staff can grant a purchased-file update or reset remaining grants with reason and audit; cannot expose the storage bucket/key or create an unlogged permanent public URL.

## Verification

Test partial shipment/refund/return combinations, parallel shipment clicks, private-file guessing, token reuse, refunded ownership, range resume, large streaming transfers and asset version replacement. Target `php artisan test --filter=Fulfillment`; browser digital delivery and staff shipping flow.

## Implementation status

T046 (2026-10-30) implements FUL-01/FUL-02 shipment creation: staff record partial or multiple shipments of paid unshipped physical quantities with optional carrier/tracking number/manual tracking URL, atomically consuming the committed stock allocation under a staff idempotency key, with overshipment prevented against shipped/cancelled/refund-held quantities. Tracking URL scheme/domain policy, customer tracking views and the shipment notification email are T047; returns (FUL-03) are T048.

T047 (2026-10-30) implements the FUL-01 tracking policy and the OPS-01 shipment notification: a `TrackingUrl` rule accepts only absolute http/https URLs on a dotted public hostname (no other scheme, no scheme-relative URLs, no embedded credentials, no whitespace/control/backslash/quote characters, no IP-literal or single-label hosts) and is enforced at shipment creation and at read time, so pre-policy stored values can never render as customer links. Shipment creation queues one deduplicated `shipment_notification_intents` row per shipment (`shipment:{shipmentId}`, mirroring the receipt intents) plus a `shipment_notification_queued` timeline event in the same transaction; the `mail.shipment` view renders only that shipment's quantities with its validated tracking link, and the customer account order page lists each shipment with its tracking link. Returns (FUL-03) remain T048.

T048 (2026-10-30) implements FUL-03 returns and deliberate restock: staff run four separately recorded steps — request (explicit quantities and reason, bounded by shipped quantities not already committed to another return), approval (per-line approved quantities; all-zero rejects the return and frees its quantities), receipt (per-line received quantities within the approved remainder; never moves stock or money) and restock (per-line restocked quantities within the received remainder, returned to `on_hand` exactly once through a dedicated allocator `restock` mutation keyed by the staff submission). Each step claims its own staff idempotency operation, so a replayed duplicate returns the existing record without a second effect and a changed payload conflicts. Returns record no money; the admin order detail shows the return summary with refund linkage — the refund facts recorded against each returned order line, read from the refund machinery — keeping approval, receipt and restock distinct from refunds as FUL-03 requires.

T049 (2026-10-30) implements the FUL-04 entitlement lifecycle: an `entitlements` table holds one durable row per digital order line, pinned to the asset version frozen on the line at purchase and snapshotting the grantee (`customer_id`, null for guest orders), with lifecycle status `active` / `suspended` / `revoked` / `expired`. An independent outbox consumer (`fulfillment.entitlements`) on `checkout.order.paid` issues through `IssueEntitlements`, which treats the event as a trigger only: it requires a recorded payment fact (captured provider payment or the local zero-total completion row) and rechecks current authoritative eligibility under order/line row locks. Eligible paid/free-completed digital lines issue `active`; fully refunded lines, cancelled orders, whole-order refunds and dispute-lost orders record a durable `revoked` tombstone instead of access, so a refund that preceded issuance can never be outrun by a replayed paid event; open-dispute or download-suspended lines record `suspended`. Issuance is idempotent per line (unique `order_line_id`), never reactivates a revoked row, never un-suspends a `suspended` download projection, and records one deduplicated `entitlements_issued` timeline entry per paid event. Download areas (T050), streaming grants (T051), refund/dispute revocation of already-issued entitlements (T052) and expiry (later slice) build on these rows unchanged.

T050 (2026-10-30) implements the FUL-04 verified download areas and the T009 historical guest-order claiming slice they depend on. Customers see downloads at `/account/downloads` for their own orders; guests reach one order's downloads at `/orders/{orderId}/downloads` only after an email verification round-trip: `VerifyGuestOrder` issues a 256-bit token bound to one guest order and its email (SHA-256 digest only, 24-hour expiry, reissue revokes prior tokens, one queued `download-access` mail intent per request), and consumption happens exclusively through an explicit CSRF-protected confirmation POST that rotates the session and stores a grant scoped to that order — scanner/prefetch GETs never consume, authenticate or mutate. Both areas expose only `active` entitlements with a `ready` pinned version (line title/SKU, asset label/version/size; never a storage key); suspended/revoked/expired rows show no files and a wrong identity sees nothing. `ClaimGuestOrders` completes T009's claiming on top of the existing `ProveCustomerIdentity`: a verified account claiming by canonical email atomically sets `orders.customer_id`, clears `guest_token_hash`, repoints unclaimed entitlements' grantee snapshot and revokes outstanding guest tokens; unverified accounts and token possession alone claim nothing. Streaming grants (T051), revocation (T052) and updates (T053) build on this unchanged.

T051 (2026-10-30) implements the FUL-05 private streaming download grants. Each entitlement carries an allowance (`download_limit`, default five per the commerce rules; `downloads_used` counter) adjustable later by T053. `AuthorizeDownload` issues a grant holding only a SHA-256 digest of a 256-bit start token bound to the entitlement, its order and the issuing principal (signed-in customer, or the verified guest scope for one order), valid five minutes; issuing spends nothing and multiple tokens may be outstanding — the allowance, not the token count, is the bound. The token is consumed only by the explicit CSRF-protected start POST: consumption rechecks the principal, the token, current entitlement/version/order eligibility and the allowance under the entitlement row lock, then atomically spends one allowance and creates a `download_transfers` session bound to the principal, the storefront session and the pinned asset version with an absolute one-hour lifetime; concurrent starts for the last allowance create exactly one transfer, and a replayed token creates nothing and spends nothing. The owner-only grant status page recovers a lost start response (it exposes the transfer without re-spending) and shows the remaining allowance; the download areas list the remaining count and the issue form. The stream route serves the pinned version's bytes from the private disk — never a redirect to a storage key — with bounded single-Range support (206 with Content-Range, 416 for unsatisfiable ranges, malformed or multi-range headers ignored), a single-stream lease per transfer, and a per-request recheck of principal, transfer state/expiry and current entitlement eligibility: revoked or suspended entitlements, revoked transfers and cross-principal requests reject new streams, an expired transfer is gone, and resume inside the hour spends no further allowance. Revocation blocks subsequent requests only; bytes already transferred are never claimed recallable. Refund/dispute revocation of issued entitlements (T052), staff grant updates/resets (T053) and the mixed-order gate (T054) build on this unchanged.

T052 (2026-10-30) implements the FUL-05 refund/dispute revocation of issued entitlements. `RevokeEntitlements::syncForOrder` recomputes every issued entitlement from the current authoritative facts under row locks — a cancelled order, a lost dispute, a whole-order refunded financial state, a revoked download projection or the entitlement's own fully refunded line revokes; an open dispute or a suspended download projection suspends; otherwise the line is eligible — and it runs synchronously inside the same transactions that record those facts: `ApplyRefundOutcome` for succeeded staff refunds (quantity, goodwill and cancellation kinds, including provider observations of staff refunds), `CancelOrder::complete` for cancellations that complete without a refund outcome (e.g. zero-total), and `ReconcileExternalAdjustment` for provider-refund success and dispute opened/won/lost effects. Because the sync recomputes from facts rather than consuming a parallel flag, both money paths converge on one rule set and replays or redeliveries are silent no-ops. Revocation is line-precise where the fact is line-precise (a fully refunded digital line revokes only its own entitlement; an unrelated physical-line refund or an amount-only goodwill refund changes nothing) and order-wide for provider refunds, which carry no line allocation — the same granularity those facts have at issuance. A transition to revoked also revokes the entitlement's unconsumed download grants and its active transfer sessions, so later starts and streams reject; suspension leaves grants and transfers in place (T051 start/stream rechecks reject on entitlement status), so a dispute won inside the transfer hour restores the same session, while a dispute won after an intervening full refund restores nothing. Each transition kind records one `entitlements_revoked` / `entitlements_suspended` / `entitlements_restored` timeline entry (counts and reasons) plus a platform audit; a sync that changes nothing records nothing. Spent download allowances are never returned by revocation (deliberate staff resets are T053), and already-transferred bytes are never claimed recallable.

T053 (2026-10-30) implements the FUL-06 deliberate staff entitlement actions. `GrantAssetUpdate` moves one entitlement's pin to a strictly newer `ready` version of the same digital asset — never the order line's frozen purchase pin, never another buyer's entitlement, and never a downgrade, a non-ready version or a different asset's version — while `ResetDownloadLimit` zeroes `downloads_used` and may replace `download_limit` inside the schema's 1..1000 bounds, rejecting a reset that would change nothing. Both actions require `fulfillment.manage`, a fresh staff two-factor session at the HTTP layer, a reason and a staff idempotency key (a replay returns the recorded result; a changed payload under the same key conflicts); both refuse terminal (`revoked`/`expired`) entitlements, so no deliberate action resurrects access the money facts removed; and each records one `entitlement_update_granted` / `entitlement_limit_reset` order timeline entry (from/to versions or previous counters, with the reason) plus a platform audit. Because a transfer binds the entitlement's pin when it starts (T051), a granted buyer streams the new version's bytes from their next download while an ungranted buyer keeps streaming the purchased version, whose file stays in place; media cleanup additionally never purges a media asset referenced by any digital asset version, at the application layer as well as through the `restrictOnDelete` foreign keys. The mixed-order gate (T054) builds on this unchanged.

T054 (2026-10-30) closes M4 with the mixed-order fulfillment gate (FUL-01/FUL-04/FUL-05/WEB-07): no production change was needed — the projections and read models already compose. `FulfillmentMixedOrder` proves it end to end over the faked provider path: one customer purchase of two tracked physical units plus one digital line issues the entitlement (`active`, version-pinned) and projects `download_status` `entitled` while the physical half stays `unfulfilled` with zero shipments, and the file genuinely downloads in that state (account area listing, grant issue, start POST, streamed bytes); a partial shipment moves only `fulfillment_status` to `partially_fulfilled`. A staff refund of one of the two physical units projects `financial_status` `partially_refunded`, records no `entitlements_revoked` or `entitlements_suspended` timeline entry, and leaves the entitlement, the download projection and a fresh streamed download untouched; shipping the remaining unit then fulfills the physical half independently. The customer order detail read model keeps the financial, fulfillment and download states separate at every step. The three Chromium journeys (digital purchase, mixed purchase, staff fulfillment) are authored in `tests/Browser/` against the real routes and UI text with their fixture preconditions recorded in-file; executing them awaits the combined PHP/Node browser harness this environment does not have (recorded since T044).
